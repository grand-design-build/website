Toronto homeowners are renovating more than ever, whether it's expanding older homes, modernizing interiors, or preparing properties for multigenerational living. With rising construction demand and limited housing supply, a well-planned renovation isn't just an upgrade; it's one of the smartest investments you can make in Toronto's real estate market.

This guide walks you through everything you need to know about home renovation in Toronto, including costs, timelines, design trends, permit rules, and how to choose the right contractor.

Why Toronto Home Renovations Are Rising in 2026

Toronto's housing landscape is changing quickly. High property values and limited lot availability mean most families are choosing to renovate instead of moving. Here's why:

  • Aging housing stock in key neighbourhoods: Most homes in and around North York were built between the 1950s and 1990s and require modernization, especially electrical, plumbing, insulation, and layout changes.
  • Demand for multigenerational living: Toronto families need basement conversions, secondary suites, larger kitchens, additional bathrooms, and barrier-free designs for seniors.
  • Rising property values: Renovating is often more cost-effective than selling and buying a new home. A well-executed renovation can increase resale value by 20–35%.

Types of Home Renovations Toronto Homeowners Are Prioritizing

Full home renovations: transforming the entire home — layout, mechanical upgrades, finishes, insulation, and energy efficiency. Best for older homes, structural issues, and beyond-cosmetic updates.

Kitchen renovations: Toronto's #1 most-requested renovation. Modern kitchens prioritize open-concept layouts, large islands, hidden storage, and high-end appliances.

Bathroom renovations: upgrades include walk-in showers, floating vanities, heated floors, and luxury fixtures from brands like TOTO, Kohler, and Rohl.

Basement renovations & legal secondary suites: demand is driven by rental income and multigenerational living. Legal basements often include egress windows, new plumbing, soundproofing, insulation upgrades, and a separate entrance.

Main-floor reconfigurations: older Toronto homes have closed-off rooms. Open-concept main floors improve resale value and family living flow.

Additions & extensions: not always full rebuilds — many homeowners add rear extensions, second-storey additions, add-a-level conversions, or interior additions within the existing footprint.

Toronto Permits & Regulations for Home Renovations

Renovations often require approvals from Toronto Building (city permits), the Committee of Adjustment (if variances are required), and Heritage Conservation (if the property is in an older, protected neighbourhood).

When you need a permit: you'll need a permit if you're doing structural changes, moving or removing load-bearing walls, basement underpinning, additions, plumbing changes, electrical changes, new basement apartments, or any work affecting life safety.

Typical permit timeline:

  • Drawings and engineering: 3–6 weeks
  • City review: 3–8 weeks
  • Committee of Adjustment (if required): 2–4 months

Home Renovation Trends in Toronto for 2026

  • Warm minimalism: soft whites, warm greys, fluted wood, natural stone.
  • Smart homes: automated lighting, voice-activated appliances, smart thermostats, and security systems.
  • Energy-efficient upgrades: heat pumps, high-performance windows, and better insulation driven by rising energy costs.
  • Spa-style bathrooms: steam showers, freestanding tubs, heated floors, aromatherapy systems.
  • Multi-purpose spaces: home offices, convertible guest rooms, play and study rooms.

How to Choose the Right Home Renovation Contractor in Toronto

Choosing the wrong contractor is the biggest risk. Here's what to look for:

  • Licensed, insured, WSIB-covered
  • In-house architectural and engineering team
  • Transparent pricing and timelines
  • Real references and portfolio
  • Familiarity with Toronto permits, zoning, and bylaws
  • Builder's warranty and post-completion support

Avoid contractors who give vague quotes, ask for large upfront payments, avoid written contracts, or can't show permits from previous jobs.

Home Renovation Process (Step-by-Step)

  • Consultation & site visit: understanding goals, budget, and feasibility.
  • Architectural & structural planning: drawings, engineering, and layout planning.
  • Permits & approvals: permit submission, zoning review, Committee of Adjustment if needed.
  • Construction: demolition, framing, plumbing and electrical, drywall, flooring, finishes, inspections.
  • Final walkthrough: quality checks and warranty documentation.

How a Renovation Can Increase Your Toronto Home Value

Renovations that bring the highest ROI:

  • Kitchen remodel: 75–100% return
  • Bathroom remodel: 60–80% return
  • Finished basement: 70–150% return (if a legal suite)
  • Main-floor layout change: 65–90% return
  • Energy upgrades: high long-term value
