Our Process

Stages of a Design And Build Project

A design-build project typically goes through several stages from concept to completion. While the exact stages may vary depending on the project and the design-build team, the following are the typical stages of a design-build project:

01

Conceptualization & Feasibility

Site visits and preliminary reviews to confirm if your vision fits within current zoning bylaws or requires a Minor Variance.

02

Designs, Permits & Zoning

We create detailed architectural drawings and 3D renderings, allowing you to walk through your home before a permit is even filed.

03

Permit Management & Pre-Construction

We identify the required permits and apply for them on your behalf. The team also finalizes the designs, and preparations for construction begin.

04

Final Inspection & Handover

Building to the approved plans and coordinating all mandatory city inspections to ensure your permit is closed successfully upon completion.

The timeline depends on the project’s complexity. For “as-of-right” projects (those that follow all zoning bylaws), the City of Toronto typically aims for a 10 to 30-day review period.

However, if your project requires a Minor Variance or a Committee of Adjustment hearing, the process can take 4 to 8 months. We manage the entire schedule to ensure no time is wasted on incomplete applications.

In Ontario, you generally need a permit if you are:

  • Increasing the building’s footprint (additions).
  • Making structural changes, such as removing load-bearing walls.
  • Changing the use of a room — for example, creating a legal basement suite.
  • Adding or significantly altering plumbing, heating, or ventilation.

Cosmetic updates like flooring, painting, or replacing kitchen cabinets usually do not require a permit.

A Minor Variance is required when your proposed design doesn’t strictly follow the local Zoning Bylaws — for example, the building is slightly too tall or too close to the property line. We help you get the necessary approvals for your dream design.

While a homeowner can technically apply, Toronto’s building codes are highly technical. Incomplete applications are often rejected, leading to months of delays.
