The Housing Stock

Living on Top of the Bluffs

Cliffcrest was carved out of farmland in the 1940s and 1950s, when the shift from streetcar suburbs to car-oriented planning brought new detached-home development to Scarborough. Most of what got built then was modest by today’s standards, one and one-and-a-half storey bungalows and side-splits on generous lots, designed for the families of that era. The lots are what stayed valuable. Frontages of 45 to 60 feet, depths often over 150 feet, and the ones closest to the Bluffs enjoying views and privacy you can’t find anywhere else in the city.

The current wave of Cliffcrest work runs in two directions. On lots where the existing home still has good bones, families are staying and renovating, often adding a second storey or rear addition to make the bungalow work as a full family home. On lots where the existing home is beyond justifying, particularly Bluff-adjacent properties where the land value dominates, teardown and custom rebuild is the answer. Both paths need the same underlying knowledge of what the site actually allows.

Good To Know

The TRCA Question

Where TRCA jurisdiction applies

▾

Any property on or near the Scarborough Bluffs, adjacent to a ravine, or within the TRCA’s regulated area needs Toronto and Region Conservation Authority approval before permits can be issued.

What TRCA reviews

▾

Slope stability, drainage, setback from the stable top of bank, tree impact, and any construction that could affect the Bluff face itself. Setbacks can range from 10 metres to over 30 metres depending on the geotechnical assessment of the specific property.

What that means for design

▾

The buildable envelope on a Bluff-adjacent lot is smaller than the lot suggests. The design has to work back from the TRCA setback, not the lot line. We know this from the start and don’t waste your time designing a home that can’t be built.

Geotechnical work

▾

Most Bluff-adjacent projects require a geotechnical report from a qualified engineer, assessing slope stability and confirming the setback recommendation. We coordinate this early in the design process.

Timelines

▾

TRCA review typically adds two to four months to the front end of the project. We build this into the schedule.
