The Danforth is one of Toronto’s most quietly consistent neighbourhoods, built out block by block between 1910 and the 1940s, largely with two-storey brick semis and detached homes that were originally working-class family housing. Today those same streets have become one of the city’s most sought-after upsize destinations, driven by the Bloor-Danforth subway line running underneath and the walkability of a genuine urban village above it. Most projects here involve renovating a century-old semi, adding rear or third-storey square footage, and increasingly, adding a laneway suite where the property backs onto a lane.

The Housing Stock

The Semi-Detached Neighbourhood

The Danforth is defined by its housing stock in a way most Toronto neighbourhoods aren’t. Semi-detached homes make up more than half of the housing sales here, most of them built between 1910 and 1940 with the same rhythm of construction: a two-storey brick semi, front porch, small front yard, narrow lot, and a long rear yard often backing onto a laneway. North of Danforth Avenue, the semis are notably consistent, with the distinctive shared front porches that give those streets their character. South of Danforth Avenue, the mix opens up to include Victorian row houses, bungalows, and newer infill.

What this means practically is that most Danforth renovations are working with a party wall on at least one side and often two. That reality shapes structural approach, mechanical routing, sound insulation, and neighbour coordination on every project. It also creates opportunities that don’t exist in fully detached neighbourhoods: coordinated renovations with neighbours, shared scaffolding costs, and additions that respect the semi’s inherent architectural symmetry.

What We Do

How We Work on the Danforth

Home Renovation

Full renovations of century-old semis and detached homes. Opening up the traditional front-to-back linear layout, kitchen and bathroom rebuilds, structural openings, electrical and plumbing replacement, and restoration of original brickwork, millwork, and architectural detail worth preserving.

Home Addition

Rear additions and third-storey additions are the most common way Danforth families gain the space they came here to find. Rear additions typically extend the kitchen and family room; third-storey additions add primary suites or additional bedrooms on top of the existing two-storey structure.

Laneway & Garden Suites

Danforth properties that back onto a rear laneway are among the strongest candidates in Toronto for laneway suites. A properly designed laneway suite adds a fully independent secondary unit that can serve as an in-law suite, rental unit, or home office.

Basement Underpinning & Finishing

Century-home basements on the Danforth typically have low ceilings and weren’t built for living. Underpinning adds the height needed to make the lower level a real family room, rental unit, or in-law suite.

Custom Build

On the rare Danforth lot where teardown makes sense, typically detached properties where the existing home is beyond justifying, custom builds designed to fit the streetscape and respect the neighbourhood’s character.

Good To Know

What Working on a Semi Actually Means

Party wall considerations

▾

Structural work on a semi affects the neighbouring home, whether through shared foundation, shared roof structure, or acoustic transmission. We plan the work to protect the neighbour’s property, coordinate any work that requires their agreement, and put dust and vibration controls in place before demolition starts.

Balloon framing and century construction

▾

Danforth semis were mostly built with balloon-frame construction, meaning wall studs run continuously from the foundation to the roof. This affects fire-blocking, structural modifications, and any electrical or mechanical routing through the walls. We handle it as part of design rather than discovery.

Original brickwork

▾

The brick facades that define the Danforth were built with soft lime mortar and specific brick types that don’t match modern equivalents. Renovations that alter the facade need masons who understand original construction, know how to source or match salvaged brick, and can restore rather than replace.

Sound isolation between semis

▾

Sharing a wall with neighbours means acoustic performance matters. Modern renovation gives us the chance to significantly improve sound isolation through resilient channels, sound-absorbing insulation, and proper detail work at the party wall interface.

Laneway suite feasibility

▾

Toronto’s Laneway Suite By-law has specific requirements around lot depth, setbacks, servicing, and access. Not every Danforth lot qualifies, but many do. We assess feasibility upfront so you know whether it’s a real option for your property.
